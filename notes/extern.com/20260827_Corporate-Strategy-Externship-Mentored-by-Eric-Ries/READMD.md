# 👉 Externship Overview

Eric Ries, author of *The Lean Startup*, teaches a diagnostic framework for evaluating companies—not just executing tasks. You’ll learn to identify financial pressures, governance problems, and mission drift, using the same framework investors and senior operators use.

## What You'll Learn and Accomplish

1. **Diagnose a Failed Company**
   - Analyze a company that failed or drifted.
   - Create an Incorruptible Scorecard + 60–90 second video/carousel.

2. **Evaluate a Company You Believe In**
   - Use the Life Pie exercise to connect your values with company selection.
   - Create an Incorruptible Scorecard and recommendation.

3. **Propose a Transformation**
   - Choose one lens to strengthen.
   - Create an action proposal and public-facing summary.

4. **Deliver Your Strategic Recommendation**
   - Build a 4-slide presentation.
   - Record a 3–4 minute video.
   - Top submissions may present live to Eric Ries.

## Who Should Join

For anyone interested in understanding why companies fail and spotting warning signs early. Useful for students interested in consulting, investment banking, PE, VC, corporate strategy, journalism, or smarter career decisions. No prior business experience required.

## Skills You'll Gain

- Diagnose company failures using the four lenses:
  - Mission Drive
  - Financial Gravity
  - Governance
  - Mission Transmission
- Read proxy statements and SEC filings.
- Recognize corruption events such as IPOs, takeovers, LBOs, activist campaigns, and founder ousters.
- Evaluate governance structures.
- Build strategic analytical scorecards.

## About the Company

*Incorruptible (2026)* is Eric Ries’s framework for building companies that resist corruption. His work includes founding and running the Long-Term Stock Exchange (LTSE). The framework comes from two decades of advising founders and focuses on the forces that can cause successful companies to lose control of their mission.

## Key Idea

**The externship teaches you how to diagnose why companies succeed, drift, or become corrupted—and how to recognize those warning signs before they become obvious.**
