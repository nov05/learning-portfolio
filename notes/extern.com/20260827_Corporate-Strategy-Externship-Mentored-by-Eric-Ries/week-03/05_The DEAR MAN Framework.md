# The DEAR MAN Framework

**A proven method for proposing ideas**

*Estimated Reading Time: 20 minutes*

---

## Why This, Why Now?

You have an idea for improving a company's commitment to being incorruptible. But good ideas fail when they're presented poorly.

The DEAR MAN framework gives you a structured way to propose ideas that are clear, persuasive, and professional. Originally from Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), this framework has been adapted for professional communication and works remarkably well for proposals and recommendations.

---

## What Is DEAR MAN?

DEAR MAN is an acronym for seven elements of effective interpersonal communication:

| Letter | Element          | What It Means                         |
| ------ | ---------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **D**  | Describe         | State the facts objectively           |
| **E**  | Express          | Share how you feel or what you think  |
| **A**  | Assert           | Make your ask or recommendation clear |
| **R**  | Reinforce        | Explain the benefits of your proposal |
| **M**  | Mindful          | Stay focused on your goal             |
| **A**  | Appear Confident | Present yourself with conviction      |
| **N**  | Negotiate        | Be open to finding middle ground      |

### Why This Works

DEAR MAN separates facts from feelings, makes the ask explicit, and shows you've thought about the listener's perspective. It's persuasive without being pushy.

---

## Breaking Down Each Element

### 1. D — Describe: The Facts

Start with objective, observable facts. No interpretation, no judgment—just what happened or what you observed.

**For your proposal, this means:**

* State the company's current situation through your chosen lens.
* Reference your Module 2 analysis data.
* Use specific examples from your research.

| Weak Description                             | Strong Description                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "The company doesn't care about governance." | "The board has no independent directors and meets only twice per year."                                                    |
| "Their mission is fake."                     | "The mission statement mentions sustainability, but their latest annual report doesn't include any environmental metrics." |
| "They have culture problems."                | "On Glassdoor, 4 of the 10 most recent reviews mention pressure to cut corners."                                           |

---

### 2. E — Express: Your Perspective

Share your interpretation, opinion, or feeling about the situation. This is where your perspective as an outside observer becomes valuable.

**For your proposal, this means:**

* Connect the facts to the *Incorruptible* framework.
* Explain what you think this means for the company.
* Use your Entry-Level Advantage—share what a fresh perspective reveals.

| Weak Expression          | Strong Expression                                                                                        |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "This is bad."           | "Based on the Four-Lens Framework, this suggests mission transmission may weaken as the company scales." |
| "They should fix this."  | "From an outsider's perspective, this creates a gap between stated values and observable behavior."      |
| "I think they're wrong." | "I believe this represents an opportunity to strengthen their commitment to incorruptibility."           |

---

### 3. A — Assert: The Ask

Make your recommendation explicit. Don't hint—state clearly what you're proposing.

**For your proposal, this means:**

* One clear recommendation.
* Specific enough to act on.
* Framed as a proposal, not a demand.

| Vague Assertion                        | Clear Assertion                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "They should do better at governance." | "I propose creating a quarterly stakeholder advisory session where employee representatives can raise concerns directly to leadership."            |
| "Fix the mission."                     | "I recommend adding a 'Mission Moment' segment to weekly all-hands meetings where one team shares how their work connects to the company mission." |
| "Improve the culture."                 | "I suggest piloting a 'Values Check' question in performance reviews for one quarter."                                                             |

---

### 4. R — Reinforce: The Benefits

Explain why your proposal matters—what's in it for the company, the stakeholders, and incorruptibility.

**For your proposal, this means:**

* Connect to the Culture Bank: What deposits does this create?
* Connect to Transmission Multipliers: How does this spread?
* Show business value, not just ethical value.

| Weak Reinforcement            | Strong Reinforcement                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| "This would be good."         | "This creates monthly deposits in the Culture Bank by giving employees a voice and showing leadership listens."          |
| "Everyone should do this."    | "Research shows that companies with strong governance practices outperform peers by 5–10% on stakeholder trust metrics." |
| "It's the right thing to do." | "By addressing this now, the company builds resilience against the governance failures that have damaged competitors."   |

---

### 5. M — Mindful: Stay Focused

Keep your eye on the goal. Don't get sidetracked by objections, tangents, or defensiveness.

**For your proposal, this means:**

* Stay focused on your one recommendation.
* Acknowledge objections without abandoning your point.
* Return to your core argument.

**Example responses to objections:**

> **Objection:** "We don't have time for this."
>
> **Mindful response:** "I understand time is precious. That's why I'm proposing something that takes only 10 minutes per week and can start with just one team."

> **Objection:** "We already do something like this."
>
> **Mindful response:** "That's great—this builds on what's already working. What I'm proposing adds a specific structure that makes the impact more visible and measurable."

---

### 6. A — Appear Confident: Credibility

Present your proposal with conviction. You've done the analysis. You've applied the framework. You have standing to propose this.

**For your proposal, this means:**

* Use declarative language, not "maybe" or "I guess."
* Reference your analysis as evidence.
* Own your outsider perspective as a strength.

| Low Confidence                           | Appropriate Confidence                             |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| "I'm probably wrong, but maybe..."       | "Based on my Four-Lens analysis, I recommend..."   |
| "This is just a student project, but..." | "From an outside perspective, I observed..."       |
| "You probably know better, but..."       | "This proposal addresses a gap I identified in..." |

---

### 7. N — Negotiate: Flexibility

Show you're open to dialogue. The best proposals invite collaboration, not compliance.

**For your proposal, this means:**

* Offer alternatives or variations.
* Acknowledge that you don't have all the context.
* Invite feedback and refinement.

| Rigid                   | Flexible                                                                    |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "This is the only way." | "This is one approach. I'm open to other ways to achieve the same goal."    |
| "Take it or leave it."  | "I'd welcome feedback on how to make this more practical for your context." |
| "You must do this."     | "What would need to change to make this workable?"                          |

# DEAR MAN in Action: Example Proposal

Here's a complete example applying DEAR MAN to a proposal.

### Company: XYZ Tech *(hypothetical)*

### Lens: Mission Transmission

### Module 2 Rating: Mixed

*Mission exists but isn't actively transmitted.*

**D — Describe**

> "XYZ Tech's mission statement emphasizes 'making technology accessible to everyone.' However, my review of the company's onboarding materials, investor presentations, and recent press releases found that the mission is only mentioned in formal documents—it doesn't appear in how employees describe their work or in customer-facing communications."

**E — Express**

> "From an outside perspective, this suggests the mission exists on paper but may not be actively transmitted to new employees or reinforced in daily work. The risk is that as the company grows, the mission becomes a formality rather than a living guide."

**A — Assert**

> "I propose creating a 'Mission Story' ritual: in weekly team meetings, one person shares a brief story (2–3 minutes) of how their work that week connected to making technology accessible. Over time, this builds a shared library of mission-in-action examples."

**R — Reinforce**

> "This creates weekly Culture Bank deposits by making mission discussions routine, not special. It leverages Transmission Multipliers by having peers, not just leadership, share stories. Research on organizational culture shows that peer-to-peer storytelling is more powerful than top-down messaging for behavior change."

**M — Mindful**

> "I know adding to meetings can feel burdensome. This is designed to be lightweight—2–3 minutes, not formal, and it can rotate so no one is always presenting."

**A — Appear Confident**

> "Based on my Four-Lens analysis and the patterns I observed in comparable companies, this addresses a real gap. I believe even a small pilot could show measurable impact on how employees talk about their work."

**N — Negotiate**

> "This could start with one team as a pilot before expanding company-wide. I'm also open to other formats—a Slack channel, a monthly email series, or an internal blog. The key is making mission discussion a regular rhythm, not a one-time event."

# TL;DR

* **DEAR MAN** = Describe, Express, Assert, Reinforce, Mindful, Appear Confident, Negotiate.
* Separates facts (**D**) from interpretation (**E**), making you more credible.
* Makes the ask explicit (**A**) and explains clear benefits (**R**), making you more persuasive.
* Stay focused (**M**), show confidence (**A**), but remain flexible (**N**).
* Use this framework to structure your action proposal.
