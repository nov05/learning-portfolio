# The DEARACT Framework

**A proven method for proposing ideas**

*Estimated Reading Time: 20 minutes*

---

## Why This, Why Now?

You have an idea for improving a company's commitment to being incorruptible. But good ideas can fail when they're presented poorly.

The **DEARACT Framework** gives you a structured way to propose ideas that are clear, persuasive, and professional. It separates facts from interpretation, makes your recommendation explicit, and encourages constructive dialogue.

---

## What Is DEARACT?

DEARACT is an acronym for seven elements of effective professional communication:

| Letter | Element       | What It Means                                           |
| ------ | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **D**  | **Describe**  | State the facts objectively                             |
| **E**  | **Express**   | Share your interpretation or perspective                |
| **A**  | **Assert**    | Make your recommendation clear                          |
| **R**  | **Reinforce** | Explain the benefits of your proposal                   |
| **A**  | **Align**     | Connect your proposal to the company's values and goals |
| **C**  | **Clarify**   | Address concerns and keep the discussion focused        |
| **T**  | **Together**  | Invite collaboration and refinement                     |

### Why This Works

DEARACT separates facts from interpretation, makes the ask explicit, connects the proposal to organizational priorities, and leaves room for collaboration.

It is persuasive without being pushy.

---

## Breaking Down Each Element

### 1. Describe — The Facts

Start with objective, observable facts. Avoid interpretation and judgment. Focus on what happened or what you observed.

**For your proposal:**

* State the company's current situation through your chosen lens
* Reference your Module 2 analysis
* Use specific examples from your research

| Weak Description                             | Strong Description                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "The company doesn't care about governance." | "The board has no independent directors and meets only twice per year."                                               |
| "Their mission is fake."                     | "The mission statement mentions sustainability, but the latest annual report does not include environmental metrics." |
| "They have culture problems."                | "Four of the ten most recent Glassdoor reviews mention pressure to cut corners."                                      |

---

### 2. Express — Your Perspective

Share your interpretation, opinion, or perspective about the situation.

This is where your perspective as an outside observer becomes valuable.

**For your proposal:**

* Connect the facts to the Incorruptible framework
* Explain what you think the findings mean for the company
* Use your Entry-Level Advantage to identify what a fresh perspective reveals

| Weak Expression          | Strong Expression                                                                                        |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "This is bad."           | "Based on the Four-Lens Framework, this suggests mission transmission may weaken as the company scales." |
| "They should fix this."  | "From an outsider's perspective, this creates a gap between stated values and observable behavior."      |
| "I think they're wrong." | "I believe this represents an opportunity to strengthen the company's commitment to incorruptibility."   |

---

### 3. Assert — The Recommendation

Make your recommendation explicit. Don't hint—state clearly what you're proposing.

**For your proposal:**

* Make one clear recommendation
* Be specific enough to act on
* Frame it as a proposal, not a demand

| Vague Assertion                        | Clear Assertion                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "They should do better at governance." | "I propose creating a quarterly stakeholder advisory session where employee representatives can raise concerns directly with leadership." |
| "Fix the mission."                     | "I recommend adding a 'Mission Moment' to weekly all-hands meetings, where one team shares how its work connects to the company mission." |
| "Improve the culture."                 | "I suggest piloting a 'Values Check' question in performance reviews for one quarter."                                                    |

---

### 4. Reinforce — The Benefits

Explain why your proposal matters—to the company, its stakeholders, and incorruptibility.

**For your proposal:**

* Connect to the Culture Bank: What deposits does this create?
* Connect to Transmission Multipliers: How does the idea spread?
* Show business value, not just ethical value

| Weak Reinforcement            | Strong Reinforcement                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "This would be good."         | "This creates regular Culture Bank deposits by giving employees a voice and showing that leadership listens."         |
| "Everyone should do this."    | "This approach can strengthen stakeholder trust by creating a consistent mechanism for employee input."               |
| "It's the right thing to do." | "By addressing this now, the company can build resilience against governance failures that have damaged competitors." |

---

### 5. Align — Values and Goals

Connect your recommendation to what the company already says it values.

**For your proposal:**

* Reference the company's mission, values, strategy, or stated priorities
* Show how your proposal supports an existing goal
* Position the recommendation as an extension of the company's own commitments

**Example:**

> "The company's mission emphasizes [X]. This proposal supports that commitment by [Y]."

Or:

> "The annual report identifies [Y] as a priority. This proposal builds on that goal by [Z]."

You're not simply criticizing the company. You're showing how it can better live up to its own aspirations.

---

### 6. Clarify — Address Concerns

Keep the conversation focused when questions or objections arise.

**For your proposal:**

* Anticipate likely concerns
* Clarify what your proposal does—and does not—require
* Return to the core objective

**Example responses:**

> **Concern:** "We don't have time for this."
>
> **Response:** "I understand time is limited. That's why I'm proposing a lightweight approach that takes only a few minutes per week and can begin with one team."

> **Concern:** "We already do something like this."
>
> **Response:** "That's a strong starting point. My proposal builds on what's already working by adding a simple structure that makes the impact more visible and measurable."

---

### 7. Together — Collaborate

The strongest proposals invite collaboration rather than demanding compliance.

**For your proposal:**

* Offer alternatives or variations
* Acknowledge that you may not have all the context
* Invite feedback and refinement
* Consider starting with a pilot

| Rigid                   | Collaborative                                                               |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "This is the only way." | "This is one approach. I'm open to other ways to achieve the same goal."    |
| "Take it or leave it."  | "I'd welcome feedback on how to make this more practical for your context." |
| "You must do this."     | "What would need to change to make this workable?"                          |

# DEARACT in Action: Example Proposal

## Company: XYZ Tech

**Lens:** Mission Transmission

**Module 2 Rating:** Mixed — the mission exists but isn't actively transmitted

### Describe

> "XYZ Tech's mission statement emphasizes 'making technology accessible to everyone.' However, my review of the company's onboarding materials, investor presentations, and recent press releases found that the mission is primarily mentioned in formal documents. It rarely appears in how employees describe their work or in customer-facing communications."

### Express

> "From an outside perspective, this suggests the mission exists on paper but may not be actively transmitted to new employees or reinforced in daily work. The risk is that as the company grows, the mission becomes a formality rather than a living guide."

### Assert

> "I propose creating a 'Mission Story' ritual: during weekly team meetings, one person shares a brief story—two to three minutes—about how their work that week connected to making technology accessible. Over time, this could build a shared library of mission-in-action examples."

### Reinforce

> "This creates regular Culture Bank deposits by making mission discussions routine rather than exceptional. It also leverages peer-to-peer storytelling, allowing employees—not just leadership—to demonstrate what the mission looks like in practice."

### Align

> "Because accessibility is already central to XYZ Tech's stated mission, this proposal strengthens an existing organizational commitment rather than introducing a new priority."

### Clarify

> "I recognize that adding another meeting activity could feel burdensome. The format is intentionally lightweight: two to three minutes, informal, and rotating so that no one person is always responsible for presenting."

### Together

> "This could begin with one team as a pilot before expanding company-wide. I'm also open to other formats, such as a Slack channel, a monthly email series, or an internal blog. The key is creating a regular rhythm around mission discussion rather than treating it as a one-time initiative."

## TL;DR

* **DEARACT = Describe, Express, Assert, Reinforce, Align, Clarify, Together**
* Start with facts, then explain your interpretation
* Make one clear recommendation
* Explain why the recommendation matters
* Connect the proposal to the company's existing values and goals
* Address concerns without losing focus
* Invite collaboration rather than demanding compliance
* Use the framework to turn your analysis into a clear, professional action proposal
