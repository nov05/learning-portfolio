# Complete Your First Incorruptible Scorecard

**Estimated time to complete: 30 minutes**

You've gathered the basics on your company. Now put it all together in the **Incorruptible Scorecard** — a visual summary of what went wrong and why.

## What You'll Do

Use your research from the previous training to fill out the interactive scorecard. By the end, you'll have a shareable graphic that captures your company's story.

This is a **first pass**. You'll deepen your analysis in Module 2. For now, focus on what you know and make your best estimates.

---

## Step 1: Open the Scorecard

Use the **Incorruptible Diagnosis Scorecard Tool** to enter your research and score your company across each lens.

If you are having trouble viewing the file on the page, use the provided link to open the scorecard tool in your browser.  
https://diagnosis-incorruptible-scorecard.netlify.app/  

---

## Step 2: Enter Your Company Info

At the top of the scorecard, fill in:

| Field                | What to Enter                              |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Company Name**     | The company you researched                 |
| **Category**         | Select the best fit, or choose **Custom**  |
| **Time Period**      | When they were active (e.g., `2010–2023`)  |
| **The "Ick Factor"** | Your one-phrase summary of what went wrong |

Use your company profile from the previous training to fill these in.

---

## Step 3: Rate the Four Lenses

For each lens, give your best-estimate rating from **1–5**.

You don't need deep evidence yet — that comes in Module 2. Go with your gut based on what you've learned so far.

### Lens 1: Mission Drive

**Could they profit while failing their mission?**

* **1 — Corrupted:** Profit came at customers' expense
* **3 — Weak:** Good intentions, but the model allowed betrayal
* **5 — Strong:** Profit required mission success

### Lens 2: Financial Gravity

**Did they resist external pressure?**

* **1 — Overwhelmed:** Investors/markets dominated every decision
* **3 — Mixed:** Some resistance, some capitulation
* **5 — Fortress:** Consistently resisted short-term pressure

### Lens 3: Governance

**Were there structural protections for the mission?**

* **1 — Failed:** One decision could (and did) undo everything
* **3 — Partial:** Some protections, but gaps remained
* **5 — Fortress:** Mission-aligned governance that held

### Lens 4: Mission Transmission

**Did they raise or lower industry standards?**

* **1 — Lowered:** Made the industry worse
* **3 — Neutral:** Little impact either way
* **5 — Raised:** Significantly improved standards

**Don't overthink it.** Your ratings will get more precise in Module 2. For now, just capture your initial read.

---

## Step 4: Add Your "Ick" Moment as Evidence

Remember the specific moment you found in the previous training. Add it to the evidence field for the **most relevant lens**.

For example:

* If your "ick" moment was about **prioritizing growth over users** → add it to **Financial Gravity**
* If it was about **a founder's self-dealing** → add it to **Governance**
* If it was about **harming customers while claiming to help them** → add it to **Mission Drive**

You can leave the other evidence fields brief or empty for now.

---

## Step 5: Write Your Diagnosis

Scroll down to the diagnosis section and complete the following:

### Root Cause

Select which lens was the **primary failure point**.

Ask yourself:

> Which weakness made everything else possible?

### One-Sentence Diagnosis

Use this template:

> **"[Company] failed/corrupted because [root cause], which allowed [specific outcome]."**

**Example:**

> "FedMart died because weak governance allowed a hostile takeover, which instantly destroyed 20 years of mission-building."

### Key Insight *(Optional)*

If you have a takeaway, add it. Otherwise, leave it for Module 2.

## 👉 **Answers for Blackboard / Anthology**

```text
BLACKBOARD / ANTHOLOGY — INCORRUPTIBLE DIAGNOSIS SCORECARD

Company Name
Blackboard / Anthology

Category
Education Technology

Time Period
1997–Present

The Ick Factor
A mission-driven education technology company became increasingly shaped by private-equity ownership, financial leverage, and acquisition-driven consolidation.

LENS 1 — MISSION DRIVE
Score: 3/5 — Weak
Could they profit while failing their mission?

Blackboard was founded around improving teaching and learning through education technology. The company's commercial success did not require ownership to preserve that mission over the long term. As ownership changed and the company became increasingly integrated into private-equity-driven acquisition strategies, financial performance and consolidation could become priorities alongside the original educational mission.

Evidence:

Blackboard grew from a mission-driven education technology pioneer into a publicly traded company and was later taken private in a 2011 leveraged buyout. Subsequent ownership changes and the creation of Anthology through multiple acquisitions shifted the company further toward a large-scale consolidation model.

LENS 2 — FINANCIAL GRAVITY
Score: 1/5 — Overwhelmed
Did they resist external pressure?

Blackboard became increasingly exposed to financial pressure after successive private-equity transactions. Leverage placed greater importance on cash generation and financial performance, while acquisition-driven expansion increased the scale and complexity of the business. The eventual debt burden became a central constraint on the company's future.

Evidence:

Blackboard was taken private in a 2011 LBO and later became part of Anthology's highly leveraged capital structure. Anthology subsequently entered Chapter 11 in 2025, illustrating how financial leverage could become a defining constraint on the company's operations and ownership structure.

LENS 3 — GOVERNANCE
Score: 1/5 — Failed
Were there structural protections for the mission?

Blackboard had little structural protection capable of preserving founder or mission-aligned control after becoming a public company. Following its 2004 IPO, extensive venture-capital dilution left co-founders Michael Chasen and Matthew Pittinsky with less than 6% of the company's stock combined. The resulting ownership structure left the company exposed to a later change in control through the 2011 private-equity buyout.

Evidence:

Blackboard's founders lost effective voting control following the IPO and subsequent dilution. The 2011 acquisition by Providence Equity Partners transferred the company from public shareholders to private-equity ownership, demonstrating how little structural protection remained for the founders' long-term mission.

LENS 4 — MISSION TRANSMISSION
Score: 2/5 — Lowered
Did they raise or lower industry standards?

Blackboard played a major role in establishing learning-management software as a core part of modern education. Its early growth helped universities and schools adopt digital teaching infrastructure at scale. Later consolidation and financial restructuring created a different trajectory, with the company increasingly organized around acquisitions, integration, leverage, and financial performance.

Evidence:

Blackboard became one of the defining LMS companies in higher education and helped establish digital learning infrastructure as an industry standard. After successive ownership changes, the business expanded through acquisitions and ultimately became part of Anthology's broader education-technology platform. The company's 2025 Chapter 11 restructuring marked the culmination of this financial and organizational trajectory.

ROOT CAUSE
    Select the primary failure point...
    Lens 1: Mission Drive
    Lens 2: Financial Gravity
    Lens 3: Governance
    Lens 4: Mission Transmission
    Multiple lenses failed together
Governance

ONE-SENTENCE DIAGNOSIS
Blackboard / Anthology became vulnerable because weak governance allowed successive private-equity owners to gain control and reshape the company around financial leverage and acquisition-driven consolidation.

KEY INSIGHT
A strong mission can survive changes in management, but it is much harder to preserve when the people committed to that mission no longer control the company's ownership structure. Blackboard / Anthology's history shows how loss of founder control can open the way for successive changes in ownership, capital structure, and strategic priorities.
```

## 👉 **Answers for Airbnb**  

```text
AIRBNB — INCORRUPTIBLE DIAGNOSIS SCORECARD

Company Name
Airbnb

Category
Travel / Short-Term Rental Marketplace

Time Period
2008–Present

The Ick Factor
Airbnb's fees and chores eroded the value that once made it a better alternative to hotels.

LENS 1 — MISSION DRIVE
Score: 3/5 — Weak

Could they profit while failing their mission?

Airbnb's mission is centered on creating belonging and making travel more accessible through unique and affordable stays. However, the company's revenue model does not necessarily require Airbnb to preserve the value proposition that originally made the platform attractive to travelers. As Airbnb increases monetization through service fees, cleaning fees, and other charges, a stay can become more expensive while guests may still be expected to perform household chores before checkout. This creates a situation where Airbnb can increase revenue even as the customer experience becomes less convenient and less competitive with traditional hotels.

Evidence:
Reports of guests paying substantial cleaning fees while still being asked to wash dishes, take out trash, or strip beds illustrate the gap between Airbnb's original value proposition and the experience some customers receive today. The platform can remain financially successful even when the combination of price, fees, and required chores makes a stay less attractive than a traditional hotel.

LENS 2 — FINANCIAL GRAVITY
Score: 3/5 — Mixed

Did they resist external pressure?

Airbnb has strong financial resources and generates substantial cash flow, giving it the ability to resist short-term financial pressure. However, as a public company, it remains exposed to investor expectations for revenue growth, profitability, shareholder returns, and continued expansion. These pressures can conflict with decisions that would prioritize long-term community interests over platform growth.

Evidence:
Airbnb's strong profitability and free cash flow provide financial independence, but public-market expectations continue to create pressure to grow the platform and increase shareholder value.

LENS 3 — GOVERNANCE
Score: 3/5 — Partial

Were there structural protections for the mission?

Airbnb has corporate governance structures and has taken steps to address issues such as trust, safety, regulation, and community impact. However, its mission is not fully protected from changes in business priorities. The company's governance structure does not make it impossible for future management or shareholders to prioritize financial performance over the original mission.

Evidence:
Airbnb has introduced policies and programs addressing safety and responsible hosting, but these protections do not eliminate the underlying conflict between platform growth and community interests.

LENS 4 — MISSION TRANSMISSION
Score: 2/5 — Lowered

Did they raise or lower industry standards?

Airbnb fundamentally changed the lodging industry, but changing the industry did not necessarily mean raising its standards. It initially became popular by offering travelers cheaper, more flexible, and more diverse alternatives to traditional hotels. Unlike hotels with relatively standardized service, cleanliness, and amenities, Airbnb relied heavily on individual hosts, resulting in a much more inconsistent customer experience.

Evidence:
Airbnb's original competitive advantage was flexibility, variety, and lower prices rather than consistently higher quality. As the platform matured, higher service and cleaning fees, combined with guest responsibilities such as washing dishes and taking out trash, further weakened its value proposition. Travelers could end up paying prices close to traditional hotels while receiving less standardized service and convenience. Airbnb expanded consumer choice, but its model did not necessarily raise—and in some respects lowered—the overall service standards of the lodging industry.


ROOT CAUSE
Financial Gravity

ONE-SENTENCE DIAGNOSIS
Airbnb was weakened by financial pressure to increase monetization, which allowed higher fees and customer burdens to erode the value proposition that originally differentiated it from traditional hotels.

KEY INSIGHT
A company can undermine its own mission without becoming unprofitable. Airbnb's challenge is that the pursuit of higher monetization can gradually erode the very value proposition that made the business successful: affordable, flexible, and convenient alternatives to hotels.
```
