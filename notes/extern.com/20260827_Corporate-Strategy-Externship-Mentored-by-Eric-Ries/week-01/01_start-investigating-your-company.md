## 👉 Task 2 Answers: Find One “Ick” Moment

**The moment that captures it:**

Airbnb guests have increasingly complained about being charged cleaning fees while still being expected to do significant chores before checking out.

One widely reported 2022 example involved an Airbnb charging a **$125 cleaning fee** while asking guests to remove the garbage, strip the bed linens, start the dishwasher, and do a load of laundry. The story went viral online and became part of a broader backlash over Airbnb “chore lists.”

A more recent 2025 case involved a vacation-rental guest who shared a **four-page cleaning checklist** that included tasks such as vacuuming, taking out the trash, and returning items to their exact positions—even though the guest had already paid a cleaning fee. The checklist reportedly warned of additional charges for failing to follow certain requirements.

This does not appear to be limited to a handful of viral stories. One analysis of more than **30,000 U.S. Airbnb listings** found that about **18% had checkout instructions**. Among those listings, about **90% charged a cleaning fee**, with an average cleaning fee of approximately **$97**, compared with $94 for listings without checkout instructions.

**Why it bothers me:**

Airbnb originally presented itself as an alternative to hotels—more affordable, flexible, and personal. But when guests pay a separate cleaning fee and are still expected to perform chores such as taking out the trash, stripping beds, washing dishes, or doing laundry, the value proposition starts to feel backwards.

I am paying for accommodation and cleaning, yet I am also spending part of my vacation doing housekeeping. At that point, the difference between an Airbnb and a hotel becomes much less attractive. A hotel may cost more upfront, but guests generally do not receive a four-page chore list before they leave.

What bothers me most is the feeling that **the customer is paying for a service while also being asked to perform part of that service**. This makes me question whether Airbnb's original value proposition has been weakened as the platform has become more commercialized.
