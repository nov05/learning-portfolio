# Analytical Compression Guide

*Estimated Reading Time: 10 minutes*

## Why this, why now?

You've done the analysis. Now you need to communicate it. This module teaches you to explain a complex corporate failure in 90 seconds or less — a skill valued in consulting, investing, journalism, and any role where you need to communicate insight quickly.

## Why 90 Seconds?

The 90-second constraint isn't arbitrary. It forces clarity:

| If You Can't Explain It in 90 Seconds            | You Probably                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| Need more than 90 seconds to explain the company | Don't understand what actually mattered       |
| Can't name the corruption event in one sentence  | Haven't identified the pivotal moment         |
| Need to explain all four lenses                  | Haven't diagnosed which one failed first      |
| Keep adding caveats and context                  | Are hedging instead of committing to analysis |

🔥 **The professional reality:** Investors, executives, and journalists make decisions based on 90-second pitches. If you can't compress your analysis, it doesn't matter how thorough it is.

---

## The Four-Beat Structure

Every strong 90-second diagnosis follows this structure:

| Beat                         | Duration | Purpose                                 | Example (WeWork)                                                                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------- | -------: | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. The Mission**           |  ~15 sec | What was the company supposed to be?    | "WeWork promised to build community through shared workspaces."                                                                                      |
| **2. The Corruption Event**  |  ~20 sec | What specific event changed everything? | "In September 2019, the IPO filing revealed chaos: billions in losses, conflicts of interest, a CEO selling his own trademarks back to the company." |
| **3. The Lens That Failed**  |  ~30 sec | Which lens was the root cause?          | "The core failure was mission drive. WeWork's stated mission — elevating consciousness — had zero connection to how it made money."                  |
| **4. The Structural Lesson** |  ~25 sec | What should have been different?        | "WeWork didn't need better PR or a different CEO. It needed a business model where success required delivering on its mission."                      |

---

## Two Examples Compared

### Example 1: Weak Evaluation

> "WeWork was a company that had a lot of problems. Adam Neumann did some questionable things and the culture was kind of toxic. They raised too much money and expanded too fast. Eventually the IPO failed because investors saw through it. There were governance issues and financial pressure and maybe the mission wasn't clear. I think they should have been more careful."

**What's wrong:**

* No specific corruption event (vague timeline)
* Lists problems instead of diagnosing
* No lens framework applied
* Hedges instead of committing ("kind of," "maybe," "I think")
* No structural lesson

### Example 2: Strong Evaluation

> "WeWork sold itself as a community company, but it was really a real estate arbitrage play. That disconnect — between stated mission and actual business model — is what killed it.
>
> In September 2019, the S-1 filing revealed the truth: $1.9 billion in losses, Adam Neumann selling his own trademarks back to the company, and a 'community company' whose CEO was extracting rather than building.
>
> The primary lens that failed was mission drive. WeWork could get rich while its members got overpriced desks. There was no structural connection between profit and purpose.
>
> The lesson: If your mission is a slide deck and not a business model, financial gravity will eventually expose the gap. WeWork didn't need a better CEO. It needed a business where success required delivering on its promises."

**What's strong:**

* Opens with clear thesis
* Specific corruption event with date and details
* Names the lens that failed and explains why
* Ends with structural lesson that generalizes

---

## The Compression Process

### Step 1: Write Everything You Know

Start by writing 500+ words about your company's failure. Get everything out.

### Step 2: Identify the Single Most Important Point

Ask: If I could only say ONE thing about why this company failed, what would it be?

### Step 3: Cut Everything That Doesn't Support That Point

* Remove caveats
* Remove "to be fair" and "on the other hand"
* Remove interesting-but-irrelevant details
* Remove everything that doesn't serve the diagnosis

### Step 4: Add the Four Beats

Make sure you have:

* One sentence establishing what the company was supposed to be
* One moment identifying the corruption event
* One diagnosis naming the failed lens
* One lesson that generalizes

### Step 5: Time Yourself

Read it aloud. If it's over 90 seconds, cut more. Every extra second weakens your analysis.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake                            | Why It's a Problem          | Fix                           |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Telling the whole story**        | History isn't analysis      | Cut to the corruption event   |
| **Blaming individuals**            | Personality isn't structure | Focus on structural failures  |
| **Listing multiple lenses**        | Doesn't show understanding  | Commit to the primary failure |
| **No specific evidence**           | Opinion isn't analysis      | Include one concrete fact     |
| **Ending with "it's complicated"** | Undermines your authority   | Commit to a lesson            |

>
> The structural lesson: [one-sentence takeaway that applies beyond this company]."
