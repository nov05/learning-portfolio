# The 4-Slide Framework

**Estimated Reading Time: 8 minutes**

## Why this, why now?

You've spent three weeks building analytical muscle: understanding the four-lens framework, evaluating your chosen company, and identifying where governance or mission alignment could improve. Now you'll pull all that work together into one focused presentation.

The format is simple by design: **four slides, one clear story**. Everyone presents the same structure, but you get to add your own personal touch.

---

## The 4-Beat Structure

Every presentation follows this general structure:

| Slide | Title          | Purpose                                        | Time Target |
| ----- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| **1** | Introduction   | Who you are and why this matters to you        | 30 seconds  |
| **2** | Your Company   | The company you evaluated and why you chose it | 45 seconds  |
| **3** | Your Scorecard | Your evaluation using the 4-lens framework     | 1–2 minutes |
| **4** | Your Action    | Which lens you'd improve and how               | 1–2 minutes |

---

## Slide 1: Introduction

**Purpose:** Tell Eric who you are and why corporate mission alignment matters to you personally.

This isn't a resume recitation. It's a 30-second story that connects your background or values to why you care about the Incorruptible framework.

### What to include

* Your name and relevant context (school, job, or career direction)
* Why you enrolled in this program
* One sentence about what you hoped to learn

### Example Script

> "I'm Jordan Chen, a junior at NYU studying finance. I enrolled in this program because I've seen how quickly companies abandon their stated values when profits drop—and I wanted to understand why some don't."

### What to avoid

* Reading your full resume
* Generic statements ("I'm interested in business")
* Skipping the personal connection

---

## Slide 2: Your Company

**Purpose:** Introduce the company you evaluated and explain why you chose it.

Eric needs context about your company—but more importantly, he wants to know your reasoning for choosing it. Your selection reveals something about your analytical interests.

### What to include

* Company name and what they do (1 sentence)
* Why you chose this company (personal connection, career interest, curiosity about a controversy)
* Any relevant context (recent news, IPO, scandal, job offer)

### Example Script

> "I evaluated Warby Parker because I'm interested in mission-driven retail and they claim to be a B Corp. I wanted to see if their 'buy a pair, give a pair' model actually holds up under the four-lens framework—especially now that they're public."

### What to avoid

* Company history dumps
* Vague reasons ("I thought it would be interesting")
* Defensive explanations for your choice

---

## Slide 3: Your Evaluation Scorecard

**Purpose:** Present your four-lens evaluation with clear grades and one-sentence findings.

This is the meat of your presentation. You're demonstrating that you can apply the framework rigorously and communicate findings clearly.

### What to include

* Rating for each of the four lenses (1–5 scale)
* One-sentence finding for each lens
* Overall assessment or pattern you noticed
* Brief mention of your process (where you found data)

### Example Structure

| Lens                 | Score | Finding                                                                   |
| -------------------- | ----: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mission Drive        |     4 | Revenue is aligned with stated mission, but growth pressure is increasing |
| Financial Gravity    |     3 | No founder control; activist investors own 12%                            |
| Governance           |     2 | Board lacks mission-protective mechanisms                                 |
| Mission Transmission |     4 | Culture still strong, but no structural protection                        |

### Example Script

> "Overall, I see a company with genuine mission intent but inadequate structural protection. If earnings disappoint, there's nothing stopping activists from forcing cost cuts that hurt the 'give a pair' program."

### What to avoid

* Reading every detail of your research
* Hedging all your grades ("It's hard to say...")
* Skipping evidence for your grades

---

## Slide 4: Your Proposed Action

**Purpose:** Pick one lens and propose a specific action to improve the company's Incorruptible health.

This is where you go from analyst to advisor. You're not just diagnosing—you're recommending.

### What to include

* Which lens you chose to focus on (and why)
* Your specific proposed action
* Why this action would work (evidence or precedent)
* What it would take to implement

### Example Script

> "I'm focusing on Governance because that's where the structural vulnerability is clearest. My proposed action: Warby Parker should adopt a dual-class share structure before their next proxy vote. This would give founders long-term control and protect the mission from activist pressure. Patagonia's trust structure shows this can work—but it requires action before a crisis, not during one."

### What to avoid

* Proposing vague actions ("They should care more about their mission")
* Choosing the easiest lens instead of the most important
* Ignoring implementation challenges

---

## Design Guidelines

Keep your slides clean and focused. Eric is listening to you, not reading your slides.

### Text

* Maximum 5 bullet points per slide
* 24pt font or larger
* No walls of text

### Visuals

* Company logo on Slide 2
* Scorecard table on Slide 3
* Keep it simple—substance over style

### Tools you can use

* Google Slides
* Canva
* PowerPoint
* Figma

**Production quality matters less than clarity.** A simple, clean deck with strong content beats a beautiful deck with weak analysis every time.

---

## What Eric Is Looking For

| Element                 | What Stands Out                                           |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Personal connection** | You explain why this matters to you specifically          |
| **Clear reasoning**     | Your company choice and grades are well-justified         |
| **Specific evidence**   | You cite sources (proxy statements, news, financials)     |
| **Actionable proposal** | Your recommendation is specific and implementable         |
| **Confident delivery**  | You own your analysis, even if uncertain about some parts |

---

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake               | Why It Hurts                                      |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Too much detail**   | You have 3–4 minutes—every second counts          |
| **No personal story** | Eric wants to know why YOU care                   |
| **Hedged grades**     | "It's kind of a B or C" shows lack of commitment  |
| **Vague action**      | "They should improve governance" isn't actionable |
| **Reading slides**    | Present to Eric, don't read to him                |

---

## TL;DR

* **4 slides, 3–4 minutes:** Introduction → Company → Scorecard → Action
* **Personal story matters:** Connect your background to why you care
* **Be specific:** Grades with evidence, actions with implementation details
* **Keep it clean:** Substance over style
* **Own your analysis:** Eric respects confidence, even with uncertainty
