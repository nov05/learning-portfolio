# Recording Your Best Take

*Estimated Reading Time: 5 minutes*

## Why This, Why Now?

You've built your 4-slide presentation. Now you need to capture it on video. This recording serves two purposes: (1) it's your submission artifact, and (2) it prepares you to present live to Eric Ries in your mentor session.

Good recording isn't about expensive equipment—it's about preparation, environment, and delivery.

## Recording Overview

| Element               | Target                                           |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Total length**      | 3–4 minutes                                      |
| **Format**            | Video with slides visible                        |
| **Face on camera**    | Yes—it makes your presentation more personal     |
| **Quality threshold** | Clear audio, readable slides, confident delivery |

## Before You Record

### 1. Rehearse Out Loud

Don't skip this. Reading your slides silently is not the same as presenting.

**Rehearsal process:**

1. Run through your entire presentation once, out loud, with slides advancing.
2. Time yourself (target: 3–4 minutes).
3. Note where you stumble or rush.
4. Run through it again, focusing on problem areas.
5. Do one final timed run.

🎯 **How many rehearsals?** 3–5 is usually right. Fewer and you'll stumble. More and you'll sound robotic.

### 2. Check Your Timing

| Slide                 |     Target Time |
| --------------------- | --------------: |
| Slide 1: Introduction |      30 seconds |
| Slide 2: Company      |      45 seconds |
| Slide 3: Scorecard    |      60 seconds |
| Slide 4: Action       |      45 seconds |
| **Total**             | **3–4 minutes** |

If you're consistently running over 4 minutes, cut content. If you're under 3 minutes, you're probably rushing or missing key points.

### 3. Know Your Key Points

For each slide, identify the **ONE thing you must say**. If you lose your place, these anchors get you back on track.

| Slide | Anchor Point                              |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Why this matters to me personally         |
| 2     | Why I chose this company specifically     |
| 3     | My overall assessment and the key pattern |
| 4     | My specific proposed action               |

## Technical Setup

### Recording Tools (Pick One)

| Tool                       | Pros                            | Cons             |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| **Loom** (recommended)     | Free, easy, shows face + slides | Requires account |
| **Zoom** (record yourself) | Familiar, good quality          | Requires setup   |
| **QuickTime** (Mac)        | Built-in, no account            | Mac only         |

🎯 **Loom is the easiest option.** It shows your face and slides simultaneously, which is exactly what we want to see.

### Camera and Audio

**Camera:**

* Built-in laptop camera is fine.
* Position it at eye level (stack books under your laptop if needed).
* Face a window for natural light—never backlit.

**Audio:**

* Built-in microphone works if your environment is quiet.
* AirPods or wired earbuds can improve audio quality.
* Avoid echo-y rooms (closets work surprisingly well).

**Background:**

* Keep it clean and uncluttered.
* A neutral wall or bookshelf is fine.
* Avoid distracting elements.

### Screen Setup

**Layout options:**

1. **Picture-in-picture (recommended):** Slides fill the screen, with your face in the corner.
2. **Side-by-side:** Slides on one side, your face on the other.
3. **Presenter view:** You are larger, slides are smaller.

🎯 **Test before recording.** Record 30 seconds and watch it back. Check audio levels, lighting, and slide readability.

## During the Recording

### Starting Strong

1. Take a breath before you start.
2. Look at the camera, not the slides.
3. Begin with confidence—your first 10 seconds set the tone.

### Managing Nerves

**Normal nervousness signs (okay to continue):**

* Speaking slightly faster than usual.
* Minor stumbles that you recover from.
* Saying "um" occasionally.

**Signs to stop and restart:**

* Complete loss of train of thought.
* Technical failure (audio cuts out, slides freeze).
* Significant stumble in the first 30 seconds.

### Eye Contact

* Look at the camera when speaking about yourself.
* Glance at the slides briefly when referencing specific data.
* Return to the camera for key points.

### Energy Level

Present like you're talking to one person across a table, not lecturing a crowd. Conversational but focused.

---

## After the Recording

### Watch It Once

Review your recording for:

* Audio is clear throughout.
* Slides are readable.
* You're visible and well-lit.
* Length is 3–4 minutes.
* No major technical issues.

### When to Re-Record

**Re-record if:**

* Audio is unintelligible.
* Slides are unreadable.
* There is a major technical failure mid-presentation.
* You significantly exceeded 5 minutes.

**Don't re-record because:**

* You said "um" a few times.
* You wish you'd phrased something differently.
* You don't like how you look.
* It wasn't "perfect."

🎯 **Authenticity beats polish.** Eric would rather see a genuine presentation with minor imperfections than an over-produced performance.

---

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                  | How to Avoid                                     |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Reading slides verbatim  | Rehearse so you know the content without reading |
| Never looking at camera  | Practice looking at the camera between slides    |
| Rushing through          | Pause between slides; let points land            |
| Apologizing for mistakes | If you stumble, continue without comment         |
| Over-producing           | Use a simple setup and authentic delivery        |

---

## TL;DR

* **Rehearse 3–5 times** before recording.
* **Use Loom** for easy face + slides recording.
* **Eye-level camera, face the light, quiet room.**
* **3–4 minutes total.**
* **Watch once; re-record only for technical failures.**
* **Authenticity > perfection.**
