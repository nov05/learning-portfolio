👉 My answers:

### 1. If your project had a highlight reel, what would be on it?

One highlight for me was finding a way to bring together perspectives that are often quite separate. SAP’s own communication around its cloud strategy is naturally very positive, while discussions on places like Reddit can be much more informal and focused on complaints. This project gave me a framework to bring the technical concerns in between those two extremes into a more structured discussion.

In particular, it helped me summarize some of the potential negative impacts of SAP’s cloud direction on customers, especially on the people responsible for the technical side of those systems, in a way that could also be understood by a broader audience.

### 2. Which skill do you feel you leveled up the most during this project? Tell us how you noticed the growth.

I think the biggest improvement was in communicating technical ideas to a non-technical audience.

I am used to thinking about enterprise systems in terms of technical details, but those details do not always translate easily into something that a broader audience can relate to. Working on this project made me more conscious of what information is essential, what can be simplified, and how a technical issue can be connected to its business or customer impact.

I noticed the difference most when I started turning fairly detailed SAP technical issues into a few clear points that could fit into a four-slide presentation.

### 3. What was the hardest part — and how did you push through it?

The hardest part was probably simplifying the technical details without losing the underlying meaning.

There are many layers behind topics such as SAP cloud migration, Clean Core, customization, APIs, and operational control. It would have been easy to either include too much technical detail or simplify the issues to the point where they became vague.

I worked through this by repeatedly asking what the actual customer impact was, and then using that as the basis for deciding which technical details were worth keeping. That helped me move from explaining how something works to explaining why it matters.

### 4. If you could tweak one part of the project to make it even better, what would you change — and why?

I think having more opportunities for feedback would have made the project even more useful.

For example, feedback from the program manager on the analysis and presentation, or comments from other participants who approached the same framework from different perspectives, could provide another useful layer of learning. It would be interesting to see where other people found an argument clear or unclear, and how they interpreted the same framework differently.

That kind of feedback could make the project feel a little more like an iterative discussion rather than only an individual exercise.
