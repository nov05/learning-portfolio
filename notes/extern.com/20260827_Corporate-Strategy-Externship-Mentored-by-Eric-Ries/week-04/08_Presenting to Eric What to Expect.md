# Presenting to Eric: What to Expect

*Estimated reading time: 5 minutes*

---

## The Mentor Session

This is your opportunity to present your analysis directly to Eric Ries. Not everyone will be selected to present live, but this guide will help you prepare for that possibility—and get the most out of watching your peers present.

---

## Format

| Detail               | Information                                    |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Duration**         | 60 minutes total                               |
| **Presentations**    | 4–6 students present live                      |
| **Time per student** | 3–4 minute presentation + 5 minutes Q&A        |
| **Audience**         | Eric Ries, PM, and your peers                  |
| **Recording**        | Session is recorded for those who can't attend |

---

## If You're Selected to Present

### Before the Session

**Preparation checklist:**

* Re-watch your recording once
* Know your slides cold—no reading
* Have your deck open and ready to share
* Test your screen share before the session
* Join 5 minutes early

🎯 **Don't over-prepare.** You've already done the work. The goal now is to be present and responsive, not robotic.

### During Your Presentation

**Opening:**

* Wait for your PM's introduction
* Share your screen
* Take a breath, then begin

**Presenting:**

* Stick to your 4 slides
* Hit your 3–4 minute target
* Look at the camera during key moments
* Let your slides support you, not replace you

**Closing:**

* End clearly: *"That's my presentation—happy to take questions."*
* Stop screen sharing
* Face the camera for Q&A

---

## If You're Not Selected to Present

You're still part of the session. Here's how to get value from it.

### During Presentations

* Watch how others structure their arguments
* Note what questions Eric asks
* Identify patterns in strong vs. weak presentations

### Take Notes On

* Questions you wish you'd been asked
* Insights from companies you hadn't considered
* How Eric thinks about the framework

### Participation

* There may be time for audience questions
* Prepare 1–2 questions for Eric or the presenters
* Quality questions are remembered

## What Makes Presentations Stand Out

From Eric's perspective:

| Element                  | What He Notices                                             |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Conviction**           | You own your analysis, even if you're uncertain about parts |
| **Specificity**          | You cite concrete evidence, not vague claims                |
| **Action focus**         | Your recommendation is implementable, not aspirational      |
| **Intellectual honesty** | You acknowledge counterarguments and limitations            |
| **Engagement**           | You're present and responsive, not performing a script      |

---

## Common Mistakes in Live Presentations

| Mistake                           | Better Approach                                 |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Reading slides verbatim           | Know the content; use slides as prompts         |
| Going over time                   | Practice until you can reliably hit 3–4 minutes |
| Defensive answers to questions    | Engage genuinely; "I don't know" is okay        |
| Apologizing for your analysis     | Stand behind your work                          |
| Not answering the actual question | Listen fully, then respond directly             |

### All Participants

* Connect with peers whose analysis interested you

* **Eric respects conviction and honesty** over polish
