# Which Lenses Matter Most to You

Estimated Reading Time: 10 minutes

### Why this, why now?

You just learned that company quality directly affects whether you can flourish. Now comes the personal question: which aspects of company quality matter most to you?

The four lenses are a diagnostic tool, but they're also a mirror. Your ranking reveals what you actually care about when evaluating a potential employer.

### Why This Self-Reflection Matters

You learned the four lenses in Week 1:

| Lens                     | Core Question                                   |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Mission Drive**        | Does profit require mission success?            |
| **Financial Gravity**    | What forces are pulling the company off course? |
| **Governance**           | What structural protections exist?              |
| **Mission Transmission** | Does the company raise or lower standards?      |

Not everyone weights these equally. Some people care deeply about working for a company with an inspiring mission. Others are more pragmatic and want to know whether governance is solid enough to protect their equity and role from a surprise acquisition.

The goal isn't to find the "right" answer. It's to find your answer: what do you actually care about when evaluating a potential employer?

### The Four Lenses Through a Personal Filter

#### Lens 1: Mission Drive

**If this lens matters most to you...**

You want your daily work to connect to something meaningful. You're not satisfied with "it pays well" as a reason to show up. You want to feel that the company only wins when it delivers on its purpose.

**You might ask in interviews:**

* "How does this role contribute to the company's stated mission?"
* "What happens when profit goals conflict with mission goals?"

**Companies that score high:** Patagonia, Novo Nordisk, Anthropic

**Watch out for:** Mission-hopeful companies that talk a good game but profit regardless of mission fulfillment.

#### Lens 2: Financial Gravity

**If this lens matters most to you...**

You understand that companies exist within a system of pressures. You want to know who's pulling the strings and whether the company can resist being pulled off course.

**You might ask in interviews:**

* "Who are your largest investors and what do they prioritize?"
* "How does the company handle pressure from analysts to hit quarterly targets?"

**Companies that navigate this well:** Costco, Vanguard

**Watch out for:** Companies with misaligned investor bases, heavy debt loads, or leadership that changes direction based on stock price.

#### Lens 3: Governance

**If this lens matters most to you...**

You think about the long game. You want structural protections that outlast any individual leader. You're skeptical of "trust me" and prefer "here's how it's enforced."

**You might ask in interviews:**

* "What voting structure protects against hostile takeovers?"
* "Does the company have public benefit corporation status or similar legal protections?"

**Companies with strong governance:** Novo Nordisk, Anthropic

**Watch out for:** Companies where founders have been ousted, single-class stock structures with no takeover defenses, or boards dominated by short-term-focused investors.

#### Lens 4: Mission Transmission

**If this lens matters most to you...**

You care about impact beyond the company walls. You want to work somewhere that makes competitors better, not worse. You think about industry effects and ripple effects.

**You might ask in interviews:**

* "How does the company influence industry standards?"
* "What happens to your suppliers, partners, and competitors because you exist?"

**Companies with positive transmission:** Costco, Patagonia

**Watch out for:** Companies whose success depends on competitors failing or who compete by lowering industry standards.

### Reflection Prompts

Complete at least one of these reflections before moving on.

**Option A: The Job Offer Test**

Imagine two identical job offers. Same salary, same role, same location. Company A scores 9/10 on your #1 lens and 4/10 on your #4 lens. Company B scores 4/10 on your #1 lens and 9/10 on your #4 lens.

Which do you take? Does this confirm or challenge your ranking?

**Option B: The Red Flag Test**

Think about your #1 lens. What would a company need to do for you to walk away from an otherwise perfect job? What's the minimum acceptable score?

**Option C: The Origin Story**

Why does your #1 lens matter so much to you? Is there an experience, a person, or a company that shaped this priority?

Remember Eric's concept of human flourishing. Your lens ranking is the first filter for identifying companies that will help you flourish.

A company that scores poorly on your #1 lens will likely leave you feeling unfulfilled, even if it excels in other areas.

* Mission Transmission matters if you care about industry-wide impact.
