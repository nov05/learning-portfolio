## Public vs. Private

**Public companies** offer easier access to verified information: proxy statements, SEC filings, financial reports, analyst coverage, and news.

**Private companies** can offer more interesting ownership and governance structures, but information is often less complete.

For a first analysis, a public company is generally easier to evaluate.

### Public Company Sources

Use [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/searchedgar/companysearch) to find:

* DEF 14A proxy statements
* 10-K annual reports
* Other SEC filings

### Private Company Sources

Look for:

* Funding announcements
* Ownership and leadership information
* Employee reviews
* Founder interviews
* News coverage

👉 My answers

我会在 SAP 和 Google 中选择其一。两家公司都处于各自技术领域的领先位置，财务状况稳定，管理体系成熟，Governance 和 Financial Gravity 都相对健康。两家公司都没有明显依赖大规模裁员、频繁重组或激进的财务操作来改善短期股价表现，因此这两个 lens 对两家公司的区分度相对有限。

两家公司最大的差异来自业务模式和技术路线。SAP 的核心是一套面向大型企业的大型 OLTP 和 OLAP 系统，交易流程复杂、业务逻辑复杂、数据关系高度复杂，对数据一致性要求很高，并发性相对有限。SAP 的技术竞争力更多来自对复杂企业业务的抽象能力、庞大系统的架构能力，以及长期积累的行业知识。它的研发动力相对小一些，因为客户主要是大型企业，技术和业务革新周期都比较长。SAP 并不需要持续创造全新的底层技术，关键更多在于识别市场上已经成熟的技术，并及时把这些技术应用到产品和服务中。因此，SAP 更依赖长期积累、稳定迭代和复杂系统管理，而不是高速技术创新。

Google 则处在完全不同的技术环境中。搜索需要支撑全球范围的大规模并发，同时公司还运营云服务、数据中心、大模型基础设施，并长期投入人工智能、量子计算等研究型项目。Google 的技术方向变化更快，研发与基础设施建设也更直接地处在技术前沿。因此，相比 SAP，Google 的 Mission Drive 和 Intellectual Fulfillment 对我而言稍强一些，技术工作的活力也更高，大型组织常见的惯性相对较弱。

从 Incorruptible 的角度看，SAP 和 Google 都是很好的案例，但两家公司体现出两种不同的长期稳定性：SAP 的优势更多来自复杂业务、系统架构、长期客户关系形成的积累；Google 则更多来自持续研发、基础设施规模和对前沿技术的投入。这样的差异也使 Google 更适合进一步观察 Mission Drive 和 Mission Transmission，而 SAP 更适合观察一家成熟大型企业如何依靠长期积累维持使命和竞争力。

I would choose either SAP or Google. Both companies are leaders in their respective technology fields, with strong financial positions and mature management. Their Governance and Financial Gravity also appear relatively strong. Neither company seems to rely heavily on large-scale layoffs, frequent restructuring, or aggressive financial measures to improve short-term stock performance. For this reason, these two lenses may be less useful for distinguishing between the two companies.

The biggest difference between SAP and Google comes from their business models and technology environments.

SAP’s core business is built around large-scale OLTP and OLAP systems for enterprises. Its systems deal with highly complex transactions, business processes, and data relationships, with a strong emphasis on data consistency and relatively less emphasis on extremely high concurrency. SAP’s technological advantage comes less from inventing completely new technologies and more from its ability to abstract complex business processes, design and maintain large-scale systems, and accumulate deep knowledge of different industries.

SAP also has somewhat less pressure for rapid technological innovation. Its customers are mostly large enterprises, whose business and technology transformation cycles tend to be relatively long. As a result, SAP does not necessarily need to invent new technologies continuously. What matters more is its ability to recognize useful technologies that already exist in the market and incorporate them into its products and services at the right time. Its strength therefore comes from long-term accumulation, stable iteration, and the ability to manage extremely complex systems rather than from constantly pursuing technological breakthroughs.

Google operates in a very different environment. Its Search infrastructure has to support massive global-scale concurrency, while the company also operates cloud services, data centers, and large-scale infrastructure for AI and foundation models. At the same time, Google continues to invest in research-oriented areas such as artificial intelligence and quantum computing. Its technology environment changes much faster, and the company is more directly involved in frontier technology development.

For me, this makes Google somewhat stronger in terms of Mission Drive and Intellectual Fulfillment. Its mission feels more inspiring, and the work environment appears to have more technological energy and dynamism. Although Google is also a very large organization with many of the challenges that come with scale, I perceive somewhat less organizational inertia compared with SAP.

From the perspective of the four lenses, SAP and Google therefore represent two different forms of long-term strength. SAP’s advantage comes primarily from accumulated business knowledge, complex systems, architecture, and long-term customer relationships. Google’s advantage comes more from continuous research, massive-scale infrastructure, and investment in frontier technologies.

This difference makes Google particularly interesting for examining Mission Drive and Mission Transmission, while SAP provides an interesting case for understanding how a mature enterprise technology company can maintain its competitiveness and long-term value through stability, accumulated expertise, and disciplined technological adoption.
