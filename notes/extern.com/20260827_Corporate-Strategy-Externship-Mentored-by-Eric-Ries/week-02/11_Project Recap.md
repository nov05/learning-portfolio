## 👉 **My Answers:**   

1. If your project had a highlight reel, what would be on it?

The biggest highlight was realizing that a huge multinational company like SAP does not really behave like a single company. Its different businesses, regions, and subsidiaries can operate almost like separate companies, while the corporation as a whole functions more like a supra-corporate entity with enormous influence over global business. I would probably analyze a company like SAP at the corporate level by looking at its degree of control over global business, while applying the Incorruptible framework more specifically to individual businesses or subsidiaries.

2. Which skill do you feel you leveled up the most during this project? Tell us how you noticed the growth.

The project inspired me to think about how business analysis could become more standardized, structured, and even quantitative. With increasingly capable tools such as large language models, it may become possible to analyze large numbers of companies and cases systematically, turning large amounts of unstructured information into structured data and potentially uncovering patterns and insights that are difficult to see from individual cases.

3. What was the hardest part — and how did you push through it?

The hardest part was dealing with information that was both unstructured and difficult to quantify. Important signals often came from earnings-call discussions, analyst questions, employee reviews, news coverage, governance details, and other qualitative sources, where the evidence was fragmented and difficult to compare systematically. I pushed through by looking for concrete, verifiable signals across multiple sources and being explicit about where the evidence supported a judgment and where it remained uncertain.

4. If you could tweak one part of the project to make it even better, what would you change — and why?

I would be interested in exploring how large amounts of unstructured information can be used to identify meaningful causal relationships. The project introduced many useful dimensions for evaluating a company, and it made me curious about how qualitative evidence from different sources could be systematically connected, compared, and eventually turned into more measurable insights.
