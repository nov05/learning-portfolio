# Mission Transmission: Do They Raise the Bar or Lower It?

**Estimated Reading Time: 20 minutes**

## Why This, Why Now?

The company you join doesn't just affect you—it affects everyone in your industry. Before you can assess whether a company is a force for good beyond its own walls, you need to understand how **mission transmission** works and what evidence to look for.

### From the Book: *Incorruptible*, Chapter 12

> Every day, millions of Americans eat safer food because of decisions made in a Costco boardroom in Issaquah, Washington. Most of them have never set foot in a Costco warehouse.
>
> Here's how it works: Costco requires every food supplier to meet safety standards that often exceed federal requirements. When one of Costco's produce suppliers installs expensive pathogen testing equipment to keep Costco's business, that same equipment tests all of the salads leaving that facility, including those sold at competing grocery stores. When Costco demands X-ray inspection systems to detect foreign objects in packaged foods, suppliers upgrade their entire production lines, not just the Costco-bound products. While only part of America's food supply flows through Costco's supply chain, these heightened safety protocols affect a far larger percentage of all food production, creating a private safety net that strengthens the entire US food system.
>
> Costco's commitment to member safety doesn't stop at their warehouse doors. It radiates outward through supply chains, competitors, and industry standards, shaping the kind of world they want their members to live in. I call this gravitational influence organizations exert beyond their own walls **mission transmission**.

# The Core Question

### Mission Transmission Lens

**Does this company raise or lower standards beyond its walls? What's one concrete example?**

This lens focuses on external impact. Even a mission-driven company with strong governance can have negative transmission if its practices drag down industry standards. Conversely, a company with weak governance might still lift an entire industry through positive transmission.

From Chapter 12:

The company you join doesn't just affect you—it affects everyone in your industry and community.

When you work at a company with **negative transmission**, you may inadvertently participate in a race to the bottom that harms workers, customers, and communities you'll never meet.

# Positive vs. Negative Transmission

| Positive Transmission                         | Negative Transmission                                  |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Company practices become industry benchmarks  | Company practices force a race to the bottom           |
| Competitors adopt better standards to compete | Competitors cut standards to survive                   |
| Employees gain skills they take elsewhere     | Industry norms degrade                                 |
| Supply chains improve to meet requirements    | Supply chains are pressured into worse practices       |
| Regulations follow the company's lead         | Regulations are needed to constrain the company's harm |

# Transmission Mechanisms

Understand **how transmission happens**.

From Chapter 12:

> "Mission-driven organizations should embrace mission transmission as a core corporate discipline, as fundamental as strategy or human resources. It's the systematic practice of ensuring that every transaction and relationship extends an organization's values beyond its own walls."

## The Five Transmission Multipliers

| Practice                                     | Description                                                                                                                   | Example                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Consider mission in every transaction** | Whenever money, power, or status changes hands, ask whether mission and ethos were considered alongside conventional factors. | Adding mission criteria to RFPs, vendor scorecards, and partnership agreements                              |
| **2. Make mission the tiebreaker**           | When all other factors are roughly equal, values alignment determines the choice.                                             | Giving small volume percentages to upstart mission-aligned vendors                                          |
| **3. Clear the path for allies**             | Actively help mission-aligned partners navigate your systems.                                                                 | Toyota sends engineers to teach smaller suppliers the Toyota Production System at Toyota's expense          |
| **4. Signal with costly commitment**         | Take actions that require real resources to demonstrate commitment.                                                           | Airbnb's commitment to compensate hosts during pandemic cancellations and its billion-dollar Host Endowment |
| **5. Create mechanisms for recourse**        | Give external partners real channels for redress when promises aren't met.                                                    | Costco's confidential ethics hotline for suppliers that routes directly to general counsel                  |

# What to Measure

Look for evidence in four domains.

## 1. Employee Treatment

| Question                          | Positive Signal                    | Negative Signal                         |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| How do wages compare to industry? | Above market; competitors matching | Below market; race to the bottom        |
| Benefits?                         | Comprehensive; imitated by others  | Minimal; industry standard lowered      |
| Turnover?                         | Low; alumni network thriving       | High; industry-wide burnout increases   |
| Work practices?                   | Sustainable; adopted by others     | Unsustainable; normalized industry-wide |

## 2. Environmental Practices

| Question                   | Positive Signal                         | Negative Signal                        |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Environmental standards?   | Above regulation; competitors following | Minimum compliance; others do the same |
| Supply chain requirements? | Pushing suppliers to improve            | Pressuring suppliers to cut corners    |
| Industry-wide effects?     | Sector carbon footprint improving       | Sector environmental impact worsening  |

## 3. Customer Treatment

| Question           | Positive Signal                       | Negative Signal                      |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Pricing practices? | Transparent; competitors adopting     | Exploitative; industry following     |
| Service standards? | High bar others must meet             | Low bar others race toward           |
| Data practices?    | Protective; setting industry standard | Extractive; normalizing surveillance |

## 4. Supplier/Partner Relationships

| Question        | Positive Signal                    | Negative Signal                           |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Payment terms?  | Fair; supporting supplier health   | Squeezing; threatening supplier viability |
| Requirements?   | Raising partner standards          | Forcing partners to cut quality           |
| Power dynamics? | Balanced; sustainable partnerships | Extractive; dependency-creating           |

# Step 3: Find Transmission Evidence

Look for signals that this company has **raised or lowered industry standards**.

### How to do this

Search for:

* "[Company] industry standard"
* "[Company] raised the bar"
* "[Company] forced competitors to"
* "[Company] race to bottom"
* "[Industry] wages after [Company]"

Academic papers and industry reports often document these effects more rigorously than news articles.

### Document

**Positive transmission signals found:**

* Competitors matching practices
* Industry awards pioneered
* Supply chain improvements
* Other evidence of standards being raised

**Negative transmission signals found:**

* Industry degradation linked to company practices
* Competitors cutting standards
* Regulatory attention
* Other evidence of standards being lowered

# Step 4: Document One Concrete Example

Find **ONE specific, verifiable example** of transmission—positive or negative.

### How to do this

Quality over quantity. Find one specific example you can cite with a source.

Examples:

* "Costco's $15 minimum wage led Target and Walmart to announce similar increases within 18 months."
* "Uber's gig model was replicated across delivery, cleaning, and logistics industries."

The best evidence shows **causation**, not merely correlation.

# Step 5: Rate Mission Transmission

Based on all your research, rate the company's industry impact.

Be honest about mixed effects. Most large companies have both positive and negative transmission.

**"Raises Standards"** means their practices become the new floor that competitors must match.

**"Lowers Standards"** means they enable a race to the bottom.

**"Mixed"** is a valid answer when evidence supports both directions in different domains.

# Example Analysis

## The Walmart Effect vs. The Costco Effect

From Chapter 12:

> "Academics call these ripples 'spillover effects' – systematic changes in local communities that result from organizational choices... These aren't separate phenomena. They're all manifestations of mission transmission."

## Company: Walmart — Lowers Standards

| Element              | Finding                                                                                                   |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Industry**         | Retail                                                                                                    |
| **Community impact** | When Walmart enters a county, local retail wages drop 0.5–1.5% per store                                  |
| **Business effects** | Business closures increase on surrounding blocks                                                          |
| **Employment**       | Total county employment falls roughly 3% within five years of a Supercenter opening                       |
| **Long-term**        | Over a decade, households in those communities earn about 6% less than comparable communities without one |
| **Rating**           | **Lowers Standards** — Creates race-to-the-bottom dynamics that hurt local economies                      |

## Company: Costco — Raises Standards

| Element               | Finding                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Industry**          | Retail / Warehouse clubs                                                                                                      |
| **Community impact**  | Unlike the Walmart Effect, Costco openings boost foot traffic and revenue for nearby businesses                               |
| **Wage transmission** | Costco's higher wages force local businesses to raise their standards                                                         |
| **Food safety**       | Costco's supplier requirements upgrade entire production lines, benefiting all grocery stores                                 |
| **Research**          | MIT's Zeynep Ton calls this the "good jobs strategy"—evidence shows it creates a rising tide rather than a race to the bottom |
| **Rating**            | **Raises Standards** — Demonstrates that mission-driven practices can lift entire industries                                  |

## Company: Tony's Chocolonely — Raises Standards Through an Open Platform

| Element                    | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Industry**               | Chocolate / Consumer goods                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Mission**                | Ending child labor and modern slavery in cocoa supply chains                                                                                                                            |
| **Transmission mechanism** | Instead of hoarding their ethical supply chain, they opened it to competitors through Tony's Open Chain platform                                                                        |
| **Concrete impact**        | By 2024, twenty mission-aligned companies sourced roughly 23,000 metric tons of cocoa through Tony's Open Chain—nearly doubling the ethical impact beyond Tony's own 17,700 metric tons |
| **Partners**               | MrBeast's Feastables, Ben & Jerry's, Albert Heijn, Aldi, and Waitrose all use Tony's Open Chain                                                                                         |
| **Rating**                 | **Raises Standards** — Transformed a moral imperative into an industry-wide platform                                                                                                    |
