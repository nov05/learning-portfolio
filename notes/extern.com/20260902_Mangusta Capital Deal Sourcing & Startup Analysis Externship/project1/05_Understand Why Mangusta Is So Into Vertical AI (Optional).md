## Why Everyone Was Betting on Vertical SaaS

Do you know that **Vertical SaaS** was one of the hottest trends in tech over the last decade?

Startups would pick a single industry — like healthcare, education, or logistics — and build super-specific software just for that sector. Over time, these tools became so good, they replaced big, clunky platforms like Salesforce or Excel.

This was called the **Vertical SaaS playbook**.

VCs like **Bessemer, a16z, and Accel** loved this model. They backed companies like **Toast, ServiceTitan, Procore, Veeva**, and more — tools that grew fast and stuck around.

## What Was the Vertical SaaS Playbook?

Here’s how great vertical startups grow into giants — and why Mangusta believes Vertical AI companies will follow a similar path, just faster and more powerfully.

```text
1. Start with a Niche

    Pick one industry that’s underserved by generic software.

    Look for:

    * Complex workflows
    * Tough regulations
    * Lots of small players (SMBs)
    * Unique or messy data

    This is your wedge—where horizontal tools like Excel or Salesforce don’t cut it.

2. Build Something They Can’t Live Without

    Create a tool that’s so perfectly tailored to that industry, it immediately beats the big platforms.

    Vertical startups win by:

    * Moving fast
    * Building only what that market needs
    * Staying close to their users

    This earns deep trust—and opens the door to bundling.

3. Re-Bundle Like a Pro

    As the company grows, it builds more products around the same customer base and bundles them into one platform.

    Why this works:

    * More revenue per customer
    * More lock-in through higher switching costs
    * More types of users across the organization

    You stop being a “tool” and become their operating system.

4. Become the Industry Default

    Once bundled, you expand broadly:

    * Serve multiple teams
    * Add features across different workflows
    * Scale globally

    This is how Stripe grew: starting with online payments, then expanding into billing, issuing, fraud detection, tax, and more.

    You become the backbone of an industry.
```

### Example: Stripe

Let’s look at an example to understand this better. Have you heard of **Stripe**?

Stripe followed this exact path. They started with a simple payment API for developers — easy to use and lightning fast.

Once they earned trust, they layered on products like Billing, Terminal, Radar (fraud), Issuing, and Tax. Each new product solved a real problem for the same customer base — until Stripe became **the financial OS for internet businesses**.
