# How to Find Startups Using Online Platforms (Core Search Stack)

*Estimated Time to Read: 10 mins*

Using AI to find startups is powerful, and real VC teams use AI too. But there’s one catch:

**If everyone uses AI the same way, everyone finds the same startups.**

Sometimes, even after trying multiple AI tools, you may still end up with zero results.

That’s why the sourcing guidance is split into two modules:

**Core Search Stack**
The primary and most reliable ways analysts find high-quality early-stage startups. Use this first, especially if AI gives you nothing.

**Secondary Hacks**
Additional creative techniques to find thesis-fit startups when the Core Search Stack isn’t enough.

## Your Core Search Stack

Use the following search sequence to find unique, early-stage startups.

### Step 1: Start With Universities and Local Accelerators

University ecosystems are useful places to find early-stage startups that may not yet appear in mainstream AI searches or databases.

Why this works:

* Founders are early-stage and active
* Founders often share updates publicly
* Startups may not yet appear in AI search results
* You may discover less obvious companies

Where to look:

* University entrepreneurship centers
* On-campus incubators
* Student hackathons
* Demo Day lists
* Innovation labs
* Local startup competitions

Search examples:

* “<University Name> accelerator startups”
* “<University Name> demo day winners 2025”

### Step 2: Use Y Combinator and Top Global Accelerators

Check startup directories from established accelerators, including:

* Y Combinator
* Techstars
* 500 Global
* Plug and Play
* StartX
* MassChallenge

For example, in the Y Combinator Startup Directory, you can search by industry, stage, region, company size, or keywords such as:

* “AI for fintech”
* “AI CRM”
* “vertical AI startup”

### Step 3: Use LinkedIn to Discover Startups in Real Time

Founders, engineers, and investors frequently post company updates on LinkedIn before startups appear in major articles, AI tools, or startup databases.

Look for:

* New company launches
* Product announcements
* Fundraising announcements
* Founder posts
* Early customer announcements
* Hiring activity

### Step 4: Use YouTube to Find Early-Stage Founders and Product Launches

YouTube can be useful for discovering early startups, particularly hardware- or engineering-heavy companies.

Founders may:

* Share product demos
* Present at competitions
* Appear at accelerator events
* Give interviews before becoming widely known
* Show prototypes publicly for the first time

Search examples:

* “vertical AI startup demo”
* “YC demo day AI pitch”
* “AI for fintech startup founder talk”
* “tech accelerator pitch day 2025”
* “vertical AI startup review”

Once you find a startup, use AI to research and verify it.

### Step 5: Use StartupHub.ai to Discover Emerging AI Startups

StartupHub.ai aggregates AI startup news, launches, and company information. It can be useful for discovering emerging AI startups that may not yet appear prominently in other sources.

## Core Search Process

The basic workflow is:

**University / Local Accelerators → YC / Global Accelerators → LinkedIn → YouTube → StartupHub.ai → AI verification**

These sources can help surface startups that a standard AI search may overlook.

If you still need more candidates, move on to **Secondary Hacks — More Ways to Find Startups**.
