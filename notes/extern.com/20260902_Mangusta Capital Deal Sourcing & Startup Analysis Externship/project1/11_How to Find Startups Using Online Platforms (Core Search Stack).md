# How to Find Startups Using Online Platforms (Core Search Stack)

*Estimated Time to Read: 10 mins*

Using AI to find startups is powerful—and real VC teams use AI too. But there’s one catch:

**If everyone uses AI the same way, everyone finds the same startups.**

Sometimes, even after trying multiple AI tools, you may still end up with zero results.

That’s why the sourcing guidance is split into two modules:

**This module: Core Search Stack**

These are the primary, most reliable ways analysts find high-quality early-stage startups. Use this first, especially if AI gives you nothing.

**Next module: Secondary Hacks**

If you still can’t find thesis-fit startups after using the Core Stack, the next module provides additional creative techniques.

## Your Core Search Stack (Use This First!)

Here is the search sequence you can use to find unique, early-stage startups.

### Step 1: Start With University & Local Accelerators

Your university ecosystem can be an underrated place to find early-stage startups that others may overlook. The same applies to other university ecosystems.

**Why this works:**

* Startup founders are early and active.
* They often post updates publicly.
* These startups may not appear in AI results yet.
* You may find unique companies for your assignment.

**Where to look:**

* University entrepreneurship centers
* On-campus incubators
* Student hackathons
* Demo day lists
* Innovation labs
* Local startup competitions

**Search examples:**

* “<University Name> accelerator startups”
* “<University Name> demo day winners 2025”

### Step 2: Use Y Combinator + Top Global Accelerators

This is another reliable place to search. Check:

* **Y Combinator (YC Directory)**
* **Techstars**
* **500 Global**
* **Plug and Play**
* **StartX**
* **MassChallenge**

On the **Y Combinator website**, click **Companies** in the top bar, then open the **Startup Directory**. You can filter by **Industry**, **Stage**, **Region**, or **Company Size**, or search by keywords such as “AI for fintech,” “AI CRM,” or “vertical AI startup.”

### Step 3: Use LinkedIn to Discover Startups in Real Time

Founders, engineers, and investors frequently post updates on LinkedIn before their startups appear in articles, AI tools, or databases.

Look for:

* Product launches
* Product announcements
* Fundraising updates
* Founder posts
* Customer announcements
* Hiring activity

### Step 4: Use YouTube to Spot Early-Stage Founders and Product Launches

YouTube can be a fast way to discover early-stage startups, especially hardware or engineering-heavy companies.

Founders often:

* Share demo videos
* Present ideas at competitions
* Appear at accelerator events
* Give interviews before they become well known
* Show prototypes publicly for the first time

**Search for:**

* “vertical AI startup demo”
* “YC demo day AI pitch”
* “AI for fintech startup founder talk”
* “tech accelerator pitch day 2025”
* “vertical AI startup review”

Once you find a startup, use AI to verify the company and assess its fit with the investment thesis.

### Step 5: Use StartupHub.ai to Discover Emerging AI Startups

StartupHub.ai is an AI-startup discovery resource that aggregates news, launches, and company information across the AI ecosystem. It can help surface new and emerging AI startups that may not yet appear widely elsewhere.

## What’s Next

You now have a core search stack for finding startups that AI may overlook:

**University & local accelerators → YC & global accelerators → LinkedIn → YouTube → StartupHub.ai → AI verification**
